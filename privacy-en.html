Privacy Policy

Papelario app (com.jrsoft.escanerdocumentos) · Last updated: 26 November 2026

In one sentence: Papelario scans, stores and converts your documents inside your phone. There are no accounts and no servers of its own, and your documents, their text and your signatures never leave your phone unless you share them. Apart from that, the only data that leaves it is the technical and advertising data collected by Google's libraries to scan, read the text and serve the ads, as explained below.

Who is responsible

The data controller is JR Soft, trading name of Javier Román Sáez, developer of the Papelario app. You can get in touch at demual@gmail.com.

What this app does NOT do

  • There is no sign-up, account, username or password for the app. You are never asked for an email address, a phone number, a name or your location.
  • It does not upload your documents. Photos, PDFs, recognized text and your signatures are processed and stored on your phone. This app has no servers of its own.
  • The app includes no analytics or crash-reporting tools of its own (no Google Analytics, no Firebase Analytics and no Crashlytics). Google's libraries do send their technical data, explained below.
  • JR Soft does not receive or store your data (except the emails you send us) and does not hand lists or databases containing it to anyone. To show and measure ads, Google's ads SDK (AdMob) collects device identifiers and other technical data on its own and sends them to Google, as explained below. Some US privacy laws consider that letting a third party's SDK collect that data for personalised advertising is “selling” or “sharing” personal data; if you live there, you can opt out (see “US residents”).
  • It does not ask for permission to use the camera, your files, your contacts or your location. Photos are taken by Google's document scanner or the system camera, and files are opened and saved through Android's own screens, only the ones you choose.
  • Documents are not included in Android's backup to Google's cloud.

Permissions

  • Fingerprint or face recognition (USE_BIOMETRIC), only if you turn on the app lock. The check is done by Android: the app never receives your fingerprint or your face, only whether it was unlocked or not.
  • Internet, network state and advertising identifier (AD_ID and the permissions of Android's advertising services), which the advertising and the Google libraries need (see below).

Data that stays only on your phone

This information is kept in the app's private storage. It disappears if you uninstall the app, if you clear its data from Android settings or if you use Settings → Delete all my data.

  • Your documents: the original photos of each page, the cropped and filtered pages, and their name, dates, folder and tags. This includes the identity documents, receipts or medical papers you scan: they are treated like any other document, only on your phone, and JR Soft does not receive them.
  • The business cards you keep in the library: the photo and its text, with the details of the person on it, and that person's or their company's name as the document name. If you scan in “Business card” mode and turn off “Also keep the card in Papelario”, the photo and its details are deleted when you leave the review. Anything else you type in “Review contact” is not kept in Papelario.
  • The recognized text of each page (including any text you correct by hand), which is used for searching and goes inside your PDFs.
  • Your saved signatures.
  • The bin: what you delete is kept for 30 days and then removed.
  • Your settings: language, theme, page format, chosen folders, lock and the other options.
  • A PDF or photo you open from another app is copied to the app's temporary storage so it can be imported.
  • A log of the app's last 10 errors (date, part of the app and type of error, with no text or document names), which only leaves your phone if you send “Report a problem”.

Backups and switching phones. Android's cloud backup (the one in your Google account) is turned off for this app's data: none of it is uploaded. Android does keep a list of the apps you had installed in your account so it can reinstall them, but Google manages that list and it contains no data from this app.

If, while setting up a new phone, you copy your data directly from the old one, with or without a cable, the app's data may be moved to the new phone. You ask for this transfer and Android carries it out, using its own tool or your phone maker's, under their own terms. According to Android's documentation, it goes from one phone to the other without going through servers. It never goes through ours: JR Soft receives nothing.

Whether it happens depends on your Android version and phone maker (it doesn't happen on Android 8 or earlier), so we can't guarantee it. If you set up the new phone from your Google account backup without the old phone, this app's data won't come back, because it isn't in that backup.

Anything that only makes sense on the old phone is not transferred, such as data encrypted with that phone's keys or your ad choices: the app will ask you again on the new phone.

What you choose to share

  • Sharing and sending by email. A document (PDF, JPG images, ZIP, Word or Excel) only leaves your phone when you share it, with the app you choose on Android's screen. If you add a password, the PDF is encrypted with AES-256.
  • Saving to your phone and the mirror folder. PDFs are saved in the folder you choose on Android's screen. If that folder syncs with a cloud (for example, Google Drive), the syncing is done by the service you chose, under its own terms.
  • Backup. It is saved as a file wherever you choose. If you add a password, it is encrypted (AES-256) and nobody can open it without the password: if you forget it, there is no way to recover it, not even for us.
  • Business cards and contacts. “Create contact” reads the name, job title, company, phone numbers, email addresses, website and address from the recognized text, on your phone, and shows them to you in “Review contact” so you can correct them. “Save to contacts” opens the phone's contacts app with those details filled in, and that app is the one that saves the contact, under its own terms (for example, if it syncs your contacts with your account). Papelario does not read your contacts or ask for permission to do so. “Share vCard” prepares the contact as a temporary file in the app's private storage, which is not kept in your library and only leaves your phone with the app you choose.
  • Clipboard. The app copies text to the clipboard only when you ask it to.
  • Read aloud. The text is read with a voice for that language downloaded to your phone, which works offline: the text does not leave your phone. If you don't have one, the app does not read the text and tells you how to download the voice in Android settings.
  • Report a problem. It opens your email app with a message that includes the app version, the phone model, the Android version, the app language and the date, part of the app and type of the latest errors (without any of your documents or text). It is only sent if you send it. If you send it to us, we receive your email address and that text; we use them only to reply to you and fix the problem, and we delete the message once the problem is resolved and, at the latest, one year after receiving it.

Other people's data. A business card, like many documents, contains another person's details. Papelario treats them like your documents: only on your phone, without sending them to JR Soft or anyone else, unless you share them. If you use them for your work or your company, and not just for yourself, you (or your company) are responsible for them under data protection law.

Data that does leave your phone

Scanner and text recognition: Google ML Kit

Photos are taken with Google's document scanner and text is recognized with ML Kit, Google libraries that run inside the phone through Google Play services (which download their models the first time). Neither the images nor the text of your documents are sent to Google. What ML Kit does send to Google is technical data about how it is working: the phone's make, model and system version, the app version, a device identifier and an installation identifier (the latter, according to Google, not intended to identify you or your phone), the configuration and size of what is processed (not its content), response times and error codes. Google uses this data for diagnostics and usage statistics for those libraries.

Arabic and Russian text is recognized with Tesseract, an open-source library that is built into the app and does not connect to the internet.

Advertising: Google AdMob

The app is free and is supported by unobtrusive Google AdMob ads, which only appear in the library and on the “Document saved” screen: never on the camera, while you edit or in full screen. Ad requests carry nothing from your documents or their text.

As with any app that uses AdMob, Google's SDK collects the Android advertising identifier (you can reset or delete it in Android Settings → Google → Ads), the app set identifier (app set ID), your IP address (from which an approximate location is inferred), your interactions with the app and with the ads, and diagnostic data. Google uses this data to serve and measure advertising, for statistics and to prevent fraud, under its own policy.

In the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland, the app shows Google's consent form before serving personalized advertising. You can change your choice at any time in Settings → Ad privacy options. If you decline, you will see non-personalized advertising (or limited advertising, without identifiers, if you also decline the use of device storage) and the app works exactly the same.

Google shows the ad consent message with wording that is common to all its advertising partners, which is why it mentions precise geolocation. According to Google, its ads SDK only uses the approximate location derived from your IP address, and this app does not give it your GPS location. You can refuse that use in “Manage options”.

How Google uses information from apps that use its services: policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites.

Ratings and Google Play

Every now and then, after you have used it successfully, the app may ask you for a rating through Google Play's official window, which is managed by Google. “Rate the app” and “More apps by JR Soft” open Google Play. To show you only the apps available in your country, “More apps by JR Soft” checks, on the phone, the country of the SIM card or of the system region; that information does not leave your phone. Its links to Google Play carry a parameter that only says they come from Papelario, with no data about you.

IP address

As with any app that connects to the internet, the Google services mentioned above receive your IP address when connecting. This app has no servers of its own, so it keeps no logs of any kind.

Legal basis and retention

Legal basis. What is processed on your phone does not reach JR Soft. For what does leave it: (1) personalized advertising and the use of device storage for advertising purposes in the EEA, the United Kingdom and Switzerland: your consent, which you give or refuse in Google's form and can withdraw in Settings → Ad privacy options (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR and Art. 22(2) LSSI-CE); (2) non-personalized advertising, its measurement and fraud prevention: the legitimate interest in funding a free app and protecting it from fraud (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR); (3) ML Kit technical data: the legitimate interest in the scanner and text recognition working and their errors being fixed (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR); (4) if you write to us (for example with “Report a problem”): the legitimate interest in replying to you and solving what you tell us about (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR). Google processes the AdMob and ML Kit data as an independent controller, under its own policies.

Data stored on your phone remains there until you delete it, uninstall the app or clear its data. This app keeps no data outside your phone, except the messages you send us, which are deleted once the problem is resolved and, at the latest, one year after we receive them; Google keeps its own data according to its policies.

Security

Your documents, their text, your signatures, your settings and temporary copies are kept in the app's private storage, which Android does not let other apps access, and they are not uploaded to the cloud backup. The app has no servers or accounts of its own that could suffer a data breach. If you turn on the app lock, opening the app requires your fingerprint, your face or your phone's PIN, pattern or password. If you add a password to a PDF or a backup, the file is encrypted with AES-256. What you save to a folder or share stays where you choose, with that place's protection. Connections made by Google's libraries (ML Kit and AdMob) are encrypted (HTTPS). The messages you send us are kept in an email account that only JR Soft can access, and are deleted within the periods stated above.

Your rights

If you live in the European Union, the GDPR grants you rights of access, rectification, erasure, restriction, portability and objection. In practice, this app keeps none of your data outside your phone except the messages you send us (for those, write to the contact address); AdMob and ML Kit data is processed by Google, with whom you can exercise your rights (policies.google.com/privacy). For what is on your phone:

  • Access and portability: share or save your documents as PDF, JPG, Word or Excel, or back up your whole library from Settings.
  • Rectification: rename your documents or correct their text in the app itself.
  • Erasure: delete a document, empty the bin or use Settings → Delete all my data; you can also uninstall the app. There is no need to ask anyone.
  • Objection and withdrawal of consent: change your advertising choice in Settings → Ad privacy options.

You can also write to the contact address or lodge a complaint with the Agencia Española de Protección de Datos (Spain's authority) or with the data protection authority of your country.

US residents

If you live in a US state with its own privacy law (for example, California, Colorado, Connecticut, Texas or Virginia), you have the right to ask that your personal data not be sold or shared for targeted advertising. You can do this in the app: Settings → Ad privacy options → “Don't sell or share my data”. From then on, you will only see non-personalized ads. The app does not send sensitive data to anyone; your documents stay on your phone. For ads, Google's SDK uses device identifiers and an approximate location (city level). For any other request about your data, write to us at demual@gmail.com.

Children

Papelario is intended for people aged 18 and over. We do not knowingly collect data from minors; the technical data sent by AdMob and ML Kit is the data described above.
